# Scope source comparison and reload

An accepted historical scope could retain its accepted heading after a Git
revision change. Shared execution correctly refused its use, but the reader
offered no comparison before the operator attempted an action.

The shell now compares the recorded full revision with its asynchronously
observed repository facts. A differing revision displays `SHA DIFFERS` in the
heading and a source hint above the body. Matching SHAs explicitly leave files
unchecked. Modified, loading and incomplete facts remain distinct. Full recorded
and observed SHA values and recorded boundary approval remain readable in detail
and plain output. The same source hint survives resize without taking body rows.

Reopening scopes and non-file scope requests refresh facts through the existing
generation-stamped worker. An already pending inspection is retained. The
navigation footer exposes `r reload` at 40 columns; acceptance and form views
retain their own controls. Descriptive source hints advertise no unavailable
subview action. Failed requests reveal their notice after paging while keeping
the composer and form drafts. Repository facts never grant permission, change
scope approval or move the workflow view. Shared source validation stays intact.

## Limits

These are last-observed facts, not continuous watching or a content-equality
certificate. A matching SHA can accompany modified files. Preparation and
execution must recheck source even after a matching hint. Historical approval
does not authorize another SHA, and a missing later finding does not establish
a fix. This fixture contains no findings and makes no finding-resolution claim.

Forty-column readers still require substantial paging for long inventory
disclosures. Human judgments remain separate from machine checks. This does
not certify every reader's source freshness, operator delight, reference-product
parity, large-repository performance, the full Rust suite or the CI/platform
matrix. No broad bounded-investigation milestone is closed.
